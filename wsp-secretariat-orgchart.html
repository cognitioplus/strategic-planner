1. Fixes that Fail: Governance Dynamics

Rapid mobilization of Job Order personnel creates short-term success but undermines long-term institutional capacity.

📊 Behavior Over Time (BOT) Graph

Oscillating peaks and declines: Performance spikes during events followed by sharp crashes as temporary staff leave. Long-term baseline remains flat or declines due to lack of permanent capacity.

💡 Key Insight: The Addiction Trap

Like an addiction, JO mobilization provides immediate relief but creates long-term dependency. Each "fix" removes pressure to implement permanent solutions, making the fundamental solution seem less urgent.

🎯 Strategic Response

Structural Integrity Ratio: Achieve 20:0 Plantilla-to-JO ratio through permanent national-regional structure.

×

2. Support vs. Priority Paradox

Borrowed staff face conflicting accountability between WSP duties and original departmental KPIs.

💡 Key Insight: Cultural Resistance

This paradox reflects cultural resistance within TESDA's bureaucracy. While leadership support is strong, institutional culture treats WSP as peripheral because staff incentives remain tied to original departments.

🎯 Strategic Response

MOP Compliance Rate: Formalize WSP roles through Manual of Operations, creating legal clarity and departmental legitimacy.

×

4. The Mobilization Trap

Speed vs. Sustainability dilemma: rapid deployment enables events but prevents lasting capacity.

📊 Behavior Over Time (BOT) Graph

Sharp capability spikes during events followed by immediate crashes as contracts end. Overall trend shows failure to build stable institutional baseline.

💡 Key Insight: Training Investment Leak

Technical training invested in JO personnel "leaks out" when contracts end, forcing the organization to restart the learning curve for every event cycle.

🎯 Strategic Response

Institutional Memory Coverage: Implement LMS to preserve knowledge independent of personnel changes, achieving 100% digital coverage of critical expertise.
